# K1 file/page-read gate (2026-09-14)

## Question

Does the local AoSoA-16/32 arithmetic advantage translate into a smaller or
more predictable read footprint for a complete K1 scan and for a candidate
gather, compared with the row-major control?

## Protocol

- the three frozen K1 sidecar seeds and the materialized row-major, AoSoA-16,
  and AoSoA-32 stores from the SIMD layout experiment are reused byte-for-byte;
- candidate gather uses the actual row-major scalar top-128 K1 addresses from
  the frozen SIMD raw evidence (not teacher IDs);
- each layout is measured for `full_scan` and `candidate_gather`, with 152
  queries, two measured passes after one untimed warm-up, and two separate
  process invocations;
- every sample reports logical payload bytes, requested file bytes, unique
  4-KiB file-page offsets, read calls, checksum, and elapsed time.

The second process invocation is a process-reopen control. Windows/OS cache
eviction is not controlled, so it must not be called a cold-disk or physical
page measurement. MDBX pages and OS physical page faults are not measured.

## Interpretation rules

`requested_read_bytes` and `unique_file_pages_4k` describe the benchmark's
actual file ranges. They are not claims about bytes fetched from storage media.
The full scan necessarily touches the whole immutable sidecar. Candidate gather
isolates the page amplification caused by row-major versus lane-major tiles;
AoSoA-16/32 may read a complete tile for one candidate because a tile is the
unit needed by the layout.

The gate is successful only when the independent audit recomputes every range,
page count, read-call count, sample identity, store hash, candidate-fixture
hash, native-output hash, and receipt aggregate. Results are component evidence
for the later K1 -> K16 -> R4 -> THQ cascade, not production activation.

## Replay result

The audit passed for 5,508 samples (three layouts, two operations, two process
reopens, three seeds, 152 candidate queries, and two measured passes; the
query-independent full scan contributes one sample per pass). First-invocation
means were:

| layout | operation | p50 ms | p95 ms | requested bytes/query | unique 4-KiB pages/query |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| row-major | full scan | 66.32 | 67.59 | 25,003,264 | 6,104.7 |
| AoSoA-16 | full scan | 65.87 | 69.26 | 25,006,080 | 6,105.3 |
| AoSoA-32 | full scan | 66.16 | 66.79 | 25,010,176 | 6,106.0 |
| row-major | top-128 gather | 1.24 | 1.41 | 49,152 | 118.6 |
| AoSoA-16 | top-128 gather | 2.89 | 3.37 | 661,491 | 206.1 |
| AoSoA-32 | top-128 gather | 3.89 | 4.61 | 1,208,967 | 295.2 |

The full scan is I/O/request dominated and shows no meaningful file-footprint
advantage for either AoSoA shape. For sparse candidate gather, the lane-major
tile is the read unit: AoSoA-16 and AoSoA-32 amplify requested bytes and page
touches relative to row-major. This is not a contradiction with the SIMD
arithmetic result: the layouts optimize a dense coarse scan, not arbitrary
single-row gathers. It argues for using AoSoA only in the sequential K1 sidecar
stage and keeping postings/candidate documents in a separate representation.
